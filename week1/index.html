Main Character Syndrome in the Marvel Universe

Every character has two numbers: how many others link to them (fame), and how many they link to (reach). The gap between those two numbers tells you exactly where you stand in the Marvel hierarchy.

TL;DR — 3 things we learned
  • Direction matters. An undirected network hides who talks about whom. Once you look at in vs out separately, four distinct social archetypes emerge from the same 303 nodes.
  • Spider-Man is a genuine hub. 106 characters reference him — that's 18× the average in-degree. Removing him leaves the network standing; removing his cluster does not.
  • Isolation ≠ obscurity. Baymax (a $650M movie franchise) is as network-invisible as Super Rabbit (a 1943 Bugs Bunny parody). Wikipedia's link graph doesn't care about box office.

Two numbers, four archetypes

In-degree = how many other characters' Wikipedia articles link to you — your gravity, your fame.
Out-degree = how many other characters your article links to — your social reach, your engagement.

Split at the median (in ≥ 3, out ≥ 4) and every character falls into one of four archetypes:

The Legends  (34) — high in, low out. Everyone references you. You reference almost no one. Genuinely famous.
Mutual Celebrities  (127) — high in, high out. Famous AND deeply embedded in everyone else's story.
Main Character Syndrome  (48) — low in, high out. They link to everyone. Nobody links back. The delusion is real.
Who Invited You  (94) — low in, low out. Barely exist in either direction.

The shape of the degree distribution

Marvel's in-degree distribution has the classic heavy tail you see in most real-world networks: most characters get referenced a handful of times, while a small elite get referenced dozens. The out-degree is more compressed — Wikipedia articles have limited real-estate — but still skewed. On a log-log axis both distributions look roughly linear, consistent with a scale-free-ish structure (Barabási, ch. 4). Switch the axes below to see it directly.

The Legends — genuinely famous, low engagement

These 34 characters have massive in-degree relative to their out-degree. The Marvel universe constantly references them; their own Wikipedia articles barely mention anyone else. Main Character Syndrome — the delusion, ranked

Main Character Syndrome is when you think you're the protagonist but the data says otherwise. The tell: high out-degree, low in-degree. You reference everyone — you're in their story, you know their name, you link to their world. But they don't link back. You're not the main character. You just act like one.

The syndrome score is out / (in + 1): high score = you reach out a lot, nobody references you back. Shamrock leads with a perfect score of 8.0 (in=0, out=8): references 8 characters and appears in zero of their articles.

This asymmetry is invisible in an undirected network. You'd see degree 8 and think: connected character. Direction reveals the truth: they are doing all the work of connection while receiving none of the credit.

The Morituri Bubble

Nine characters form a completely separate connected component. They are all from The Morituri, a 1980s storyline. Inside their bubble, Radian is their Spider-Man (in-degree 4). Zero links cross into the 277-character giant. They are quarantined — not forgotten, but invisible.

Who Invited You — the Forgotten 17

Seventeen characters have degree zero: no links in, no links out. They exist in the Wikipedia category but are completely invisible in the network. Most surprising: Baymax, the robot companion from Big Hero 6 (over $650M at the box office), is as disconnected as Super Rabbit — a 1943 Bugs Bunny parody character. In network terms, fame in the real world means nothing here.

What surprised us

Three things we did not expect

1. Baymax is invisible. A robot from a billion-dollar franchise has the same network profile as a 1943 Bugs Bunny parody. Wikipedia editors don't cross-link cross-medium characters.

2. Spider-Man is not the linker — he's the linked. He points to only 9 characters, but 106 point to him. His Main-Character Score (10.6) is ten times the average. He is the archetype we now know as a Legend, not a Mutual Celebrity.

3. Whole storylines can be network islands. The Morituri bubble — 9 tightly linked characters from one 1980s arc — has zero edges to the giant component. Community detection didn't find them; graph traversal did.

Method & data

  • Data: The frozen Week 1 snapshot of the Marvel Comics superheroes Wikipedia category (303 characters, 1,784 directed wiki-links) provided with the course.
  • Tools: Python / NetworkX for graph analysis and community detection; D3.js v7 for every visualisation on this page (rendered from network.json + charts.json).
  • Community detection: Greedy modularity maximisation (nx.community.greedy_modularity_communities) on the undirected projection — 25 communities.
  • Isolated nodes preserved: All 17 zero-degree characters are kept in every count and every visualisation.
  • Reading: Barabási, Network Science, ch. 2 (Graph theory) and ch. 4 (Scale-free property).
  • Source: github.com/simonesskovgaard/Social-Interactions-and-Graphs
