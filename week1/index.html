Main Character Syndrome in the Marvel Universe

Every character has two numbers: how many others link to them, and how many others they link to. Plot those two numbers and a hidden hierarchy appears — stars, glue, pillars, and ghosts.

The question

The dataset is a directed Wikipedia link network: 303 Marvel superheroes, with an edge A → B whenever A's Wikipedia article links to B. We loaded all 303 nodes before adding edges — otherwise the 17 characters with zero connections would silently disappear.

The obvious question is: who matters most? The obvious answer is in-degree — Spider-Man has 106, Hulk has 64, Wolverine has 60. But that's only half the story. Every character has two numbers, and the relationship between them is where things get interesting.

Two numbers, four archetypes

In-degree measures how many others link to you — your fame, your gravity, how much the universe orbits around you.
Out-degree measures how many others you link to — your social reach, how embedded you are in everyone else's story.

Split on the median (in ≥ 3, out ≥ 4) and four archetypes emerge:

The Stars — Main Character Syndrome in its purest form

These 34 characters have high in-degree but low out-degree. The universe talks about them constantly, but their own Wikipedia articles barely mention anyone else. They are the gravitational centres — everything orbits around them, but they don't orbit back.

The extreme case is Ms. Marvel: 11 characters link to her, but her own article links to just 1. She has a Main Character Score (in / (out + 1)) of 5.5 — the universe discusses her far more than she discusses the universe.

The Glue — the unsung heroes

These 48 characters link to many others but are rarely linked back. They appear in everyone's story but never get top billing. The most extreme examples are genuinely surprising:

Shamrock and Isaiah Bradley have zero in-degree. Their Wikipedia articles reference 8 and 7 other Marvel characters respectively — but not a single character's article ever links back to them. They connect others to the network while being completely invisible themselves. This is the inverse of Main Character Syndrome: the Support Character Curse.

The Main Character Score

We defined Main Character Score as in-degree / (out-degree + 1). A high score means you receive far more attention than you give — the purest expression of Main Character Syndrome.

The surprise: Spider-Man is technically a Pillar (high in, high out) — his out-degree is 9, above the median of 4. But his MCS of 10.6 is by far the highest in the network. The gap between 106 in and 9 out tells the real story.

The Morituri Bubble — a parallel universe

Nine characters form a completely separate connected component, with zero links to or from the 277-character giant component. They are all from The Morituri, a 1980s Marvel storyline about heroes who gain superpowers at the cost of their own lives. Their Wikipedia articles reference each other extensively — but the wider Marvel Wikipedia ecosystem has completely ignored them.

Inside their bubble, Radian is their Spider-Man — the most-linked character with an internal in-degree of 4. He is unknown to the 277 characters in the main universe. He has never been linked to. He doesn't exist to them.

The Forgotten 17 — not even a quadrant

Seventeen characters have degree zero: no links in, no links out. They exist in the Wikipedia category "Marvel Comics superheroes" but are never mentioned by name in any other superhero's article, and their own articles mention no one else. They don't belong to any quadrant.

The most striking case is Baymax — the robot companion from Big Hero 6, a Disney movie with over $650 million at the box office. His Wikipedia page exists, but it apparently links to no other Marvel superhero, and no Marvel superhero's page links to him. In network terms, he is as disconnected as Super Rabbit (a 1943 Bugs Bunny parody character).

What we asked, what we built, what surprised us

We asked Claude Code to design the analysis from scratch: load the data, classify characters into quadrant archetypes, compute the Main Character Score, and build this site. The agent suggested the quadrant frame unprompted — we just told it the in-degree/out-degree asymmetry was interesting.

The Shamrock finding genuinely surprised us. A character can be active in the network (linking to 8 others) while being completely invisible (zero in-degree). This is only possible in a directed network — an undirected analysis would have given her degree 8 and hidden the asymmetry entirely.

The Morituri we expected to be boring background characters. Finding that they form a self-contained parallel universe with its own internal hierarchy — Radian as their local celebrity, completely unknown outside — felt like discovering a hidden room.
