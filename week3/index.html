Six Degrees of Spider-Man (It's Actually Three)

"Six degrees of separation" is the famous claim for social networks in general. In the Marvel wiki-link network, every reachable character is at most three hops from Spider-Man. We built a tool to show the exact chain for any character, then went looking for the single most surprising bridge in the whole network, checked properly against 200 degree-preserving shuffles.

TL;DR: 3 things we found
  • Three, not six. Spider-Man reaches all 277 characters in the giant component in at most 3 hops, which makes sense once you know he's also the network's center (radius 3) from week 3's distance measures.
  • Rockman is the most surprising character in the network, by far. Betweenness z-score of +13.45 against a degree-preserving null. The next closest character isn't even half that. He has exactly two links.
  • It's not about how many links Rockman has. It's who's on the other end. One of his two neighbors is a character whose only link in the entire 303-character network is back to Rockman. He's the sole door to an entire pocket of the wiki.

Everyone's closer than you'd think

Spider-Man has degree 106 in the undirected giant component: over a third of every other tracked character links to or from him directly. That single fact does most of the work: 106 characters are one hop away, another 152 are two hops away (a friend of a friend), and only 18 characters sit at the maximum distance of three. Nobody in the reachable network is a stranger for long. This isn't a coincidence: Spider-Man is also the network's center (the node with the smallest eccentricity, radius 3), so by definition nothing can be farther from him than the radius itself.

The most surprising bridge, checked properly

"Surprising" needs a baseline, or it's just an anecdote. We ran 200 degree-preserving shuffles of the giant component, the same double-edge-swap procedure from exercise 3.8, recomputed betweenness centrality after every shuffle, and turned each character's real betweenness into a z-score: how many standard deviations above the shuffled average their real position is. A node whose betweenness is purely a byproduct of its degree should land near z = 0. A node sitting in a structurally unusual spot stands out.

Top 15 characters by betweenness z-score against 200 degree-preserving shuffles. Rockman's z-score of 13.45 is more than double the runner-up.

Why Rockman, specifically

Rockman has degree 2, one of the least-connected characters in the giant component, yet his betweenness centrality (0.0072) already beats most 15-20-link characters outright, and against the null model it's a runaway outlier. The reason is entirely structural, not about popularity:

Rockman's two neighbors are Black Widow (degree 25, well embedded in the core of the network) and The Witness, whose only link anywhere in all 303 characters is back to Rockman. Every shortest path that needs to reach The Witness from anywhere else in the network has no choice but to pass through Rockman first. Two links is enough to be indispensable, if one of them is the only door into an otherwise sealed room.

That's also why degree-preserving shuffling is the right null here rather than just comparing raw degree ranks: a random rewiring that keeps Rockman at degree 2 almost never happens to route The Witness's one link through him specifically, so the real network's betweenness for Rockman towers over what chance alone predicts.

Method & data

  • Data: the same frozen Week 1 snapshot (303 characters, 1,784 directed / 1,434 undirected wiki-links) used throughout this site.
  • Giant component: largest connected component of the undirected projection, 277 of 303 characters.
  • Paths: single-source BFS rooted at Spider-Man on the undirected giant component (networkx.single_source_shortest_path), precomputed once and shipped as static JSON. No client-side graph traversal needed.
  • Null model: 200 independent degree-preserving double-edge-swaps per exercise 3.8's method (networkx.double_edge_swap, 5×|E| swap attempts each), betweenness recomputed after every shuffle.
  • z-score: (real betweenness minus mean of 200 shuffles) / (standard deviation of 200 shuffles), per node.
  • Tools: Python / NetworkX for all graph computation; D3.js v7 for the chart; the six-degrees tool is a plain lookup against a precomputed path table.
